# Photographic realism — physical and editorial coherence

Load when photographic appearance, realistic lighting/camera or CGI-like defects are central. Prefer scene-specific causal explanation over arbitrary "8K ultra-real HDR" adjectives.

## Physical coherence checklist

- **Camera:** viewpoint, height (only if known), orientation, field of view and lens perspective; preserve fixed geometry/camera references over photographic style suggestions.
- **Lighting:** time of day, principal light source direction/size, ambient fill, softness and plausible exposure; contact shadows agree with ground and object placement.
- **Reflectance:** glass/metal/water reflections must correspond to visible surroundings, sun/sky and camera angle; translucent/transparent surfaces remain plausible.
- **Tone/color:** consistent white balance, restrained contrast, believable bright and dark detail, atmospheric depth; avoid uniform over-sharpening, excessive local clarity and synthetic saturation.
- **Focus:** architectural documentation often needs extended depth of field; use selective blur only if the shot genuinely calls for it. Focal length influences field of view; camera position influences perspective.
- **Environment:** weather and season should match landscape, clothing and light; human figures must have credible scale, contact and occlusion.

## Realism without contrived flaws

Do not add film grain, chromatic aberration, scratches, dirt, haze, vignettes, lens distortion or motion blur simply as a realism recipe. Such characteristics are optional, intentional editorial choices, and can hide architectural details. Most realistic outcomes come from consistent illumination, materials, perspective, texture scale and compositing.

## Photographic vs architectural goals

Prioritize: (1) user-authorized architectural geometry and message, (2) physical plausibility, (3) editorial quality. A dramatic golden-hour style may conflict with neutral documentation; a wide field-of-view cue must not conflict with the provided camera. For local photo edits preserve exposure, contrast, saturation and white balance outside the targeted region unless user requests global relighting.

## Concise working vocabulary

"Soft directional daylight with coherent building/contact shadows; controlled exposure preserving sky and façade detail; natural material reflectance; realistic depth separation and restrained color grading." Replace generic clauses with observed conditions as available. Avoid describing camera settings as externally verified EXIF data.

## Evaluation

Look for impossible or multiple conflicting shadows, floating objects, flat/uniform materials, misplaced highlights, implausible sky reflections, super-wide distortion, identical people/vegetation, exaggerated HDR and crunchy over-sharpening. Diagnose one causal category at a time, while respecting the approved base.
